# Behavioural Tips for Reducing Household Electricity Use

*Authored for this project — Sustainability Intelligence DSS*

## Small Habits, Measurable Impact

Behavioural changes do not require capital investment. The following actions have been validated in UK energy efficiency programmes and randomised trials.

## Lighting and Appliance Use

- **Switch to LED lighting**: LEDs use 75–85% less electricity than incandescent bulbs with equivalent output. A household fully switching to LED can save 150–300 kWh/year.
- **Full loads only**: Running a dishwasher or washing machine with a full load rather than a half load reduces energy use per item washed by 30–50%.
- **Lower wash temperatures**: Washing clothes at 30°C instead of 60°C uses roughly 40% less electricity per cycle.
- **Boil only what you need**: The kettle is one of the most used appliances in UK homes. Boiling only the water needed saves 20–40 kWh/year.
- **Fridge and freezer settings**: The recommended fridge temperature is 3–5°C and freezer is -18°C. Going colder increases energy use by 5% per extra degree.

## Monitoring and Awareness

Research from the UK and US consistently shows that households provided with real-time or near-real-time consumption feedback reduce their electricity use by 5–15% compared with control groups. In-home displays, smart meter apps, and energy dashboards are effective feedback mechanisms.

## Social Comparison

Seeing how your consumption compares to similar households (same ACORN group, same tariff, similar house size) is a powerful motivator. Households that are above the median for their peer group are more likely to take action when shown the comparison.

## High-Impact vs Low-Impact Actions

High-impact: heating behaviour, EV charging timing, appliance replacement.
Low-impact: phone charger standby, LED night lights, turning off unused lights.

Focus energy advice on high-impact actions first for measurable results within the data window.
