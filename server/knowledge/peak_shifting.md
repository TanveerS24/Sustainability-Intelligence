# Peak Load Shifting for Household Electricity

*Authored for this project — Sustainability Intelligence DSS*

## What Is Peak Load Shifting?

Peak load shifting means deliberately moving high-energy tasks from periods of peak grid demand to off-peak periods. In the UK, the evening peak typically runs from 4 pm to 8 pm on weekdays, when the grid serves both commercial and residential demand simultaneously.

## Why It Matters

During peak periods, grid operators must run additional peaking plant — often gas turbines — to meet demand. These plants have higher carbon intensity than base-load sources such as nuclear or wind. Shifting load away from the peak reduces both the need for peaking plant and the marginal carbon intensity of your electricity.

## Practical Actions

### Large Appliances
- Schedule dishwashers and washing machines to run after 8 pm or before 7 am.
- Use the delayed-start feature on tumble dryers.
- Electric vehicle charging should be set to overnight (11 pm – 6 am) using built-in timers.

### Space and Water Heating
- Pre-heat water in the morning so the tank is hot before the evening peak.
- Homes with heat pumps can pre-heat rooms slightly before 4 pm so the system does less work during peak hours.

### Smart Home Systems
- Smart plugs with scheduling can automate appliance timing.
- Home energy management systems can respond to half-hourly pricing signals if on a Time-of-Use tariff.

## Expected Savings

Studies from the UK Smart Grid Forum found that households shifting 15–20% of evening peak demand could reduce peak-period CO₂ emissions by 8–12% per household-day, depending on the generation mix that day.
