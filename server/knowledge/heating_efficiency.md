# Heating Efficiency and Electricity Consumption

*Authored for this project — Sustainability Intelligence DSS*

## Why Heating Dominates Winter Consumption

Space heating is the single largest end-use in most UK homes, accounting for roughly 60–65% of total household energy use. In homes that use electric heating — including heat pumps, electric panel heaters, or storage heaters — this translates directly into elevated electricity consumption during cold periods.

## Temperature and Consumption Correlation

Analysis of the London smart meter dataset shows a clear inverse relationship between outdoor temperature and household electricity consumption. On cold days (below 8°C), average hourly consumption can be 30–50% higher than on warm days (above 16°C) for heating-reliant households.

## Heat Pumps vs Resistive Heating

- **Resistive heating** (panel heaters, fan heaters, electric boilers): Coefficient of Performance (COP) ≈ 1.0 — every kWh in produces 1 kWh of heat.
- **Air-source heat pumps**: COP typically 2.5–3.5 — every kWh in produces 2.5–3.5 kWh of heat by extracting energy from outdoor air.
- **Ground-source heat pumps**: COP typically 3.5–5.0 in UK conditions.

Switching from resistive to heat-pump heating can reduce electricity consumption for heating by 60–70% while maintaining the same comfort level.

## Practical Efficiency Actions

- **Insulation**: Loft, cavity wall, and floor insulation reduce heat loss and therefore the energy required to maintain indoor temperature.
- **Draught-proofing**: Gaps around doors and windows account for 15–25% of heat loss in older UK housing stock.
- **Thermostat control**: Reducing setpoint temperature by 1°C typically reduces heating energy by 8–10%.
- **Zonal control**: Heating only occupied rooms reduces unnecessary load.
- **Pre-heating**: For households on ToU tariffs, pre-heating before the peak period and letting the house cool slightly during the expensive window reduces peak draw.
