# Standby Power and Always-On Devices

*Authored for this project — Sustainability Intelligence DSS*

## The Hidden Load

Standby power — electricity consumed by devices that are switched off or in sleep mode — accounts for an estimated 9–16% of total household electricity use in developed countries. In the UK, this corresponds to roughly 300–600 kWh per year per household.

## Common Standby Offenders

| Device | Typical Standby Power (W) | Annual kWh (if always on) |
|---|---|---|
| Desktop computer (sleep) | 2–5 | 18–44 |
| Set-top box / DVR | 10–20 | 88–175 |
| Games console (standby) | 1–15 | 9–131 |
| Microwave (clock only) | 2–5 | 18–44 |
| TV (standby) | 0.5–3 | 4–26 |
| Router / modem | 6–12 | 53–105 |

## The Night-Heavy Load Profile

Households with a "night-heavy" consumption pattern often have a high baseline driven by always-on devices, servers, or electric storage heaters charging overnight. Identifying and eliminating unnecessary standby loads is the most impactful intervention for this cluster.

## Practical Actions

- Use smart plugs with power monitoring to identify high standby consumers.
- Switch off devices at the wall when not in use.
- Replace older set-top boxes and DVRs with modern equivalents — standby power has improved significantly in recent generations.
- Enable "auto power-off" features on TVs and monitors.
- For network equipment, consider a scheduled router restart or managed switch with port scheduling if night-time internet use is zero.
